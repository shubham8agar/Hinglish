# Hinglish Language Guide

This guide describes the current writing direction for content across technology, education, finance, law, health, public services, and everyday life. It is a shared starting point, not a fixed or universal standard.

## Writing principles

### Keep the language natural

Use Hindi sentence structure and phrasing that people would comfortably say or read. Avoid translating English sentence structure word for word. Prefer familiar, straightforward Hindi over formal vocabulary chosen only to make a sentence look more translated.

### Translate the sense, not the wording

Carry the source's intended meaning and effect into Hinglish rather than matching each word or sentence structure. Preserve the author's voice, tone, emphasis, politeness, humor, and emotional weight where they matter. Adapt idioms and metaphors when a literal version sounds unnatural or changes the meaning. Do not add claims, urgency, reassurance, or certainty that the source does not contain. If context does not resolve an ambiguity, flag it for review instead of guessing.

**Example**

- English: Let's get the ball rolling.
- Word-for-word: चलो गेंद को लुढ़काना शुरू करें।
- Sense-first: आइए, काम शुरू करते हैं।

### Use Devanagari for Hindi

Write Hindi words in Devanagari. Keep English technical words, product names, commands, and standard abbreviations in Latin script when that makes them easier to recognize. For example: `Backup`, `Cloud Server`, `Password Manager`, `VPN`, and `2FA`.

### Keep useful domain terms in English

Retain an English term when it is commonly used in the relevant field, appears in an interface or source material, or is more recognizable than a Hindi equivalent. Examples include `Backup`, `Interest Rate`, `Deadline`, and `Two-Factor Authentication`. Do not retain English just to sound technical. If a term may be unfamiliar, explain what it means in plain language nearby.

### Preserve meaning and safety

Keep the source meaning, facts, level of certainty, and warnings intact. Do not simplify away a prerequisite, limitation, or risk. Ask a qualified subject-matter reviewer to check content where incorrect wording could cause harm, data loss, or other serious consequences.

### Respect variation

Hindi usage differs across regions and communities. Use accessible phrasing, avoid mocking or correcting a reader's dialect, and consider alternatives when a word may not be widely understood. Where useful, note the audience or region a phrase is intended for.

### Match the domain and risk

Use vocabulary readers encounter in their field, but do not assume that one audience knows every English term. Preserve the source's facts, numbers, units, citations, qualifications, deadlines, and warnings. For law, health, finance, safety, and other high-impact subjects, translation does not replace qualified review and must not be presented as professional advice.

## Example

आज के Digital दौर में अपने Personal Data को Secure रखना बहुत ज़रूरी है। Cyber Criminals अक्सर Phishing Links और Malware के ज़रिए आपके System में Hack करने की कोशिश करते हैं। इसलिए, अपने सभी Online Accounts पर Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) ज़रूर On रखें। इसके अलावा, हर Website के लिए एक अलग और Strong Password का Use करें, जिसे Manage करने के लिए आप किसी Trusted Password Manager की Help ले सकते हैं। Public Wi-Fi का Use करते समय बिना VPN के कोई भी Banking Transaction न करें, क्योंकि इससे आपकी Sensitive Information Leak होने का Risk बढ़ जाता है। अपने Phone और Laptop के Operating System और Apps को समय-समय पर Update करते रहें ताकि पुराने Security Bugs को Fix किया जा सके।

This is a style example, not a verified security guide. Technical instructions should be checked for accuracy and kept current before publication.

## Reviewing an example

When reviewing a contribution, consider:

- Is the meaning technically correct and complete?
- Does it preserve the source's intent, voice, tone, and emotional effect without adding or removing emphasis?
- Does the Hindi read naturally in Devanagari?
- Are English terms familiar or useful in this context?
- Are unfamiliar terms explained without making the text harder to read?
- Would the intended audience understand the instruction and its consequences?
